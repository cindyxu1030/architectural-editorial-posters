# Poster grammar

## The recurring system

The reference family combines architectural photography or rendering with editorial typography and interface-like annotation. Its strongest common trait is spatial integration: graphic elements appear attached to, behind, inside, or wrapped around the building.

## Composition motifs

- **Building as grid:** floors, windows, rails, and seams define alignment.
- **One oversized headline:** condensed sans, high-contrast serif, or a restrained brand display face.
- **Controlled type collision:** a secondary italic or script phrase crosses the main title, but does not replace it.
- **Depth layering:** type can pass behind a balcony, emerge from a facade, or sit inside a glass volume.
- **Technical frames:** thin rectangles, corner nodes, connector lines, percentages, or diagram fragments.
- **Architectural path:** one curve, ramp, orbit, ribbon, or circular instrument guides the eye.
- **Data as texture:** small labels and numbers support the world-building. They do not carry essential claims.
- **Asymmetry with anchors:** compositions feel dense but are stabilized by a clear title block, edge alignment, or frame.

## Image characteristics

- strong geometric architecture with visible repetition or curvature
- clear foreground, midground, and background separation
- dramatic upward, downward, or oblique perspective
- room for type in sky, wall, glass, shadow, or facade bands
- controlled atmosphere: haze, bloom, reflections, foliage, clouds, or night color
- enough local contrast to separate overlays from the image

Avoid generic real-estate photography, flat elevations with no depth, or scenes filled edge to edge with detail.

## Typography

### Scale and integration

- Measure reference typography against the visible artwork bounds. Exclude phone UI, player controls, black bars, device frames, and mockup margins before comparing scale.
- In this reference family, the combined primary title commonly occupies roughly one-quarter to two-fifths of the artwork height and spans most of the available width. Treat this as a calibration range, not a template.
- Make the title share the building's spatial system through facade-scale sizing, edge alignment, architectural cropping, selective occlusion, curvature, reflection, or material treatment.
- Avoid placing the entire title on a large generic dark rectangle. A panel is justified only when it is clearly derived from a real facade plane, glass bay, shadow volume, or structural opening.
- Use this removal test: if the architecture can disappear and the headline still reads as a complete standalone title card, the typography is not integrated enough.

Use a maximum of two expressive type voices plus one neutral information face, unless a brand system is stricter.

- Main title: large, compressed, extended, or high-contrast. It owns the first read.
- Secondary phrase: lighter, italic, serif, or script. Use it once.
- Information text: neutral sans, small, compact, and aligned to a grid.
- Microtype: optional atmosphere only. Never hide dates, venues, prices, or calls to action here.

Large type may crop at the edge when the word remains recognizable. Do not crop dates, names, or calls to action.

## Color systems

Choose one base and one accent logic:

- charcoal, white, acid green
- midnight blue, cyan, signal yellow
- grayscale, vermilion
- white, pale gray, coral or dusty pink
- cool white, cyan, orange

Use the accent on the main title, one path, or selected labels. Do not scatter accent color evenly. If a brand kit applies, derive the contrast logic from the kit instead of importing these palettes.

## Linework and panels

- 1 to 3 line weights only
- translucent panels at roughly 8 to 22 percent opacity
- visible corner anchors or nodes where useful
- borders may break or disappear behind architectural layers
- perspective lines must agree with the image or remain clearly diagrammatic
- charts and percentages need real meaning when presented as data; otherwise keep them abstract and unlabeled

## Density control

Allocate visual attention roughly as:

- 55 to 70 percent architecture and atmosphere
- 15 to 25 percent title system
- 5 to 15 percent diagram or path
- 5 to 10 percent labels and microtype

These are composition checks, not hard quotas. Remove microtype and extra panels first when the frame becomes crowded.

## Anti-patterns

- random cyberpunk HUD pasted over a building
- five unrelated display fonts
- giant text that ignores the architecture
- meaningless English filler presented as real information
- even distribution of boxes, lines, and color
- literal one-to-one copying of a reference poster
- source creator marks or fake branding
- final text entrusted entirely to an image generator
